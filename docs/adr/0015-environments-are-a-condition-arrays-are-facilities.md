# A gas environment is a condition, an array is a facility, and the units behind both run whole

Two mechanisms of the game act on an area, not through a port. A Gas Dispersing Unit (`vaporizer_1`) takes 6 a minute of a gas and sets that gas's **gas environment** around itself, which five recipes need in place. A Forge of Luminance (`magic_spawner_1`, v1.7.0) takes 6 Xiragen a minute and projects a 5 × 5 **array** in front of itself; every 10 s each Cuprium or Xiranite on a belt inside it has a chance to turn into its L, T or E form. Arrays of one kind stacked completely tier up (L and T: T1 to T4, 20 / 40 / 60 / 100%); an L and a T stacked make an E-Array (T1 and T2, 40 / 80%). The Bipolar Forge (`magic_spawner_2`) projects two arrays for the same 6 a minute.

Neither fits a link: an environment isn't consumed and serves every facility in range, and where an area lies is layout (ADR 0002). So:

- **The units run whole.** The Gas Dispersing Unit and both forges are facilities marked `whole_units`. A recipe of one takes its gas and puts out nothing; each of its inputs is an activation input of which nothing scales (ADR 0013), and its facility count is a whole number: the largest the limit and the supply allow (a limit of 1.5 runs 1). Fed by links it is settled by pass 1's whole-number search as ADR 0013 has it, and measured by its inputs (ADR 0004), so it is a goal like a sink. On the depot it runs `floor(limit)`. The picker places one with a limit of 1: how many units a plan needs is the player's to say.
- **A gas environment is a condition, not a flow.** A recipe names the gas it needs (`environment`, since ADR 0014); a Gas Dispersing Unit's recipe names the gas it spreads (`creates_environment`). The recipe node's card says so with a label by the facility name ("Stable Environment"). The solver runs the recipe either way and warns (`EnvironmentMissing { node, gas }`) when no node of the graph that creates that gas runs at least one unit.
- **An array is a facility, one per tier**: `magic_ming_1` to `_4` (L-Array T1 to T4), `magic_hui_1` to `_4`, `magic_heng_1` and `_2`, from the game's formation ID and level. Each of the six conversions is one recipe (the game's craft ID at level 1, such as `formation_magic_ming_item_copper_ming_1`) that every tier of its array can run: 25 in and 25 out per 10 s at speed 1, one for each tile of the area, with the tier's chance as the facility's speed. So one L-Array T1 converts 30 a minute and a T4 150; an E-Array T1 60, a T2 120. It is an ordinary recipe node, linear in the solver, and its facility switch picks the tier.
- **Projections are counted.** A forge's recipe says what it projects (`projects`: the T1 array's facility ID and how many), and it has one recipe for each choice of arrays: L or T for the Forge; L + L, L + T or T + T for the Bipolar. An array facility says what it is stacked from (`stack`: `magic_ming_3` is three `magic_ming_1`; `magic_heng_1` is one `magic_ming_1` and one `magic_hui_1`). The solver adds up, over the graph, what the array nodes need (each node's count rounded up, times its stack) and what the forge nodes project, and warns where a projection falls short (`ProjectionsShort { projection, needed, provided }`). Unlike an environment's range, stacking is exact, so this count is too.
- **The format stays `1`.** The new keys (`whole_units` and `stack` on a facility, `creates_environment` and `projects` on a recipe) are optional, so every file written before them still loads.

## Considered Options

- **Xiragen as an activation input of the array itself, with no forge nodes.** Rejected: one card fewer, but the gas an array costs depends on which forges project it. A T2 array is two Forges (12 a minute) or one Bipolar (6), and the game hands out Bipolar Forges in limited number.
- **Count the belt tiles inside an array.** Rejected: that is layout (ADR 0002). The array's rate is the ceiling of a fully belted area instead, and the facility count times 25 reads as the tiles a plan needs.
- **Stop a recipe whose environment is missing.** Rejected: the app can't know which unit covers which facility, so tying the two would need the player to say, and a rule that switches recipes off (a whole number for each) in every pass.
- **An environment or a projection as an item on a link.** Rejected: an environment is not consumed, and a projection would be an item the game doesn't have, with a rate that isn't items per minute.
- **A recipe per tier under the game's four craft IDs** (`…_copper_ming_1` to `_4`). Rejected: 20 recipes in the browser for six conversions, and changing the tier would mean swapping the recipe and its links. The tier belongs to the array, which is what a facility is here.

## Consequences

- **An array's rate is a ceiling**: every one of its 25 tiles holding an item not yet converted, every pulse. Below 100% the belt leaves the area mixed, so a real line filters and loops the rest back; the graph shows the net flow.
- Read from the tables and not yet seen running in the game, so listed in `data/README.md` as assumptions: one pulse every 10 s reaches every item in the area; an E-Array T2 is two L and two T projections (the tables only say L + T makes E); `consumeRate` 6 is per minute and `consumeRateUpperLimit` 30 only caps the intake, as on the Gas Dispersing Unit.
- The game lists the six conversions as crafts of the Forge of Equilibrium (`magic_balancer_1`), four or two copies each for the tiers. `aic-extract` takes them off it: that facility keeps its three real recipes.
- A recipe node whose environment is missing still shows its numbers. The warning is the only sign besides the label, and how many dispersing units a plan needs stays with the player.
- Both warnings look at the whole graph, workspaces included. Every recipe node that needs an environment is checked, whatever its count; a unit counts as running when its count is at least 1 or its section is undetermined. A projection with any of its array or forge nodes undetermined is not checked.
- Arrays count in the totals' facilities like any other; rounded up, that is the number of arrays to project. They have no power draw.
- `data/v1.6.0.json` gains `whole_units` and `creates_environment` on its Gas Dispersing Unit by hand: its tables are no longer on hand to extract again. A graph saved with a dispersing unit at a fractional count now runs it whole.
- A limit typed as a rate through a unit's port is the largest count whose flow fits it, as ADR 0013 already has it.
